The layer that sits **on top** of a finished name — and the rules for naming
things that aren't people. Read this when adding a byname to an existing

## The four byname constructions

### 1. The definite byname — `[Name], the [Adjective] [Noun]`

> Balerion, **the Black Dread** · Logen, **the Bloody Nine**

The noun should be **slightly wrong for a person**. A Dread is a feeling. A Nine
is a number. That wrongness is the entire effect — it tells you the world stopped
thinking of them as a person.

*Nouns that work:* Dread · Nine · Quiet · Verdict · Hour · Tithe · Question ·
Answer · Winter · Debt · Kindness · Mercy · Errand · Remainder · Wound · Hush ·
Sum · Season · Argument

*Nouns that don't:* Warrior · Destroyer · Slayer · Champion · Conqueror. These
name a *job*, and a job is not frightening.

*Adjectives:* Black · Bloody · Pale · Unlit · Slow · Patient · Last · Second ·
Drowned · Laughing · Unburied · Quiet · Salt · Long · Thin · Kind

> **The number trick.** Logen the Bloody Nine is the strongest byname in the
> influence list because a number implies a story you are never told. Nine what?
> Steal this: the Third Silence, the Ninth Kindness, Eleven Winters, the Fourth
> Dondarrion.

### 2. The courtly title — `[Object or rank] of the [Abstract or time]`

> **Lord of the Morning** · Sword of the Evening · Keeper of the Last Hour

Reads as an *office* that existed before this holder and will outlive them. Use
when the character inherited something.

*Openers:* Lord · Lady · Sword · Shield · Hand · Voice · Eye · Warden · Keeper ·
Bearer · Herald · Mouth · Ledger · Lantern
*Closers:* the Morning · the Evening · the Last Hour · the Long Table · the
Drowned Coast · the Quiet Season · the Unanswered · the Ninth Gate · the Tide

### 3. The compound byname — `[Word][Word]`, no article

> Kaladin **Stormblessed** · Origajima **Foldspire** · Dondarrion **Ravensway**

Full treatment in `styles.md` §3. The rule worth repeating: make the second half
*gentler* or *stranger* than the first. Stormblessed, not Stormbringer.

### 4. The nobiliary particle — `[Given] [particle] [Surname]`

> Sand **dan** Glokta · Lan **al'**Mandragoran

The particle encodes *what kind of world this is*. Decide what yours means and
apply it consistently — **dan** might mark a fencing rank, **al'** a crown in
exile, **ven** a landed house, **ap** a bastard line. One particle per culture.

*Particles:* dan · ven · ap · el · al' · ter · zo · na · vor · dis · sur · di

## Blunt titles

The corpus puts short Anglo-Saxon titles in front of long exotic names:
*King* Damarion Chimamandara, *Priest* Dondarrion Ravensway. The contrast
anchors a floating name and tells you the character's function in one word
before the exotic part arrives.

Single-syllable and two-syllable titles that carry weight: King · Priest ·
Warden · Reeve · Marshal · Abbot · Steward · Sergeant · Doctor · Judge ·
Master · Goodman · Mother · Elder · Captain · Bailiff · Sexton

Avoid stacking a blunt title *and* a byname *and* a three-part name — pick two.

## House and clan names

Houses follow the same styles as people, with one adjustment: **a house name
should sound like it could also be a place**, because it usually was one.

- **Anglo-Gothic houses:** House Grimsditch, House Sallowmere, House Wainbarrow.
  The toponymic style is already house-shaped.
- **Westerosi cut:** doubled consonants + **-ion / -yn / -ys**: Dondarrion,
  Vaellys, Corrayne, Marroth.
- **Imperial Rolling clans:** long and echoing: the Chimamandara, the Adesolanaro.
  Use the definite article and drop the word "House."
- **Apostrophe-Clan septs:** Ashe'kani, Vash'endi. Here the sept element *is*
  the house, so people carry it as a surname already.

**The plural trick.** Refer to a house as a plural people — "the Grimsditches,"
"the Chimamandara" — and it stops being a heraldic label and becomes a family
with cousins you don't like.

## Orders, circles, and cults

Formula: `The [Adjective or Number] [Collective Noun] of [Thing]`, then drop any
part that sounds redundant.

*Collectives:* Order · Circle · Chapter · Choir · Hand · Watch · Ledger ·
Congregation · Lantern · Thread · Quorum · Sept · Tally · Ash

*Examples:* The Quiet Ledger · The Ninth Watch · The Ashen Choir · The Thread of
Vesper · The Unlit Chapter · The Tally of Drowned Men · The Long Congregation

**The best order names are bureaucratic.** A cult called The Quiet Ledger is
more frightening than one called The Bloodfang Brotherhood, because a ledger
means they're *writing your name down*.

## Named objects

Swords, staves, relics. Two approaches, and they're opposites:

**A — the grand.** Portmanteau-Root or Sindarin: Narsil, Dawnbreaker, Gurthang.
Build from `phonetics.md` roots: nár (fire) + thang (oppression) → Narthang.

**B — the domestic.** Name it something small and ordinary, which is far more
unsettling: *the Kindness* · *Little Sister* · *the Argument* · *Nettle* ·
*the Last Word* · *Tuesday* · *Mother's Own*.

Approach B is underused and lands harder at a table, especially on a weapon that
has done something terrible. Offer at least one when naming a blade.

## Gods and powers

Gods get **two names**: what their faithful call them, and what they are.

> **Seshen-of-the-Unbroken-Water**, whom the fishers call the Patient One.

- Formula: `[Concept-Loan root]` + `-of-the-` + `[Sentence-Name fragment]`.
- The common name should be plain, short, and slightly *too* gentle: the Patient
  One, the Good Neighbour, the Quiet Mother, Him Below, the Long Guest.
- For eldritch or outer powers, use `Sentence-Name` (styles.md §7) directly —
  **That-Which-Counts-The-Doors** is a better outer god than any
  apostrophe-laden invention, because it tells you what it *does to you*.
