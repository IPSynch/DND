# The Corpus, Dissected

Every name the author has actually used, plus their stated influences, broken
down into what makes each one work. Read this when:

- the user asks **why** a name works, or wants an existing name critiqued;
- you need to match a **new character to an existing one** (same family, same
  culture, same campaign);
- a slate of candidates keeps missing the author's taste and you need to
  recalibrate against ground truth.

The pattern notes at the end are the highest-value part of this file.

---

## The author's own characters

### Male

**Bartholomew Grimsditch** — *Aberrant Mind psion.*
`Anglo-Gothic`. A parish-register given name over a real English earthwork-name
(Grim's ditch, Cheshire). The style's entire engine is the collision: the most
mundane possible name attached to a man whose mind is not his own. The
possessive **-s-** in Grim**s**ditch is doing heavy authentication work — it's
what real toponymic surnames do.

**Dalligar Detresschal** — *Warlock, power soul & darkness.*
`Portmanteau-Root` twice over. *Dalligar*: doubled **ll** for weight + **-gar**,
the Germanic "spear" element found in Edgar, Ansgar, Holger. *Detresschal*:
French *détresse* (distress) + **-chal** on the pattern of *maréchal* /
*seneschal* — a court office. So: a spear-man holding an office of distress.
Note how the doubled consonants (ll, ss) slow the name down and make it heavy.

**King Damarion Chimamandara** — *80-year-old king and paladin.*
`Imperial Rolling`. Chimamanda is Igbo — "my personal spirit will never be
broken" — which is exactly a man still mowing through demons at eighty. The
*-ra* extends it into a dynasty. Five open syllables with the **ma** repeated;
nothing in the name ever stops, which is why it sounds like it's been announced
for four hundred years. The blunt Anglo-Saxon title *King* in front provides
contrast and keeps it from floating away.

**Yuddha Rama Samsara** — *20-something master swordsman turned fisherman.*
`Concept-Loan` at three parts. Sanskrit: *yuddha* = war, *Rama* = the exiled
warrior-king, *samsara* = the wheel of death and rebirth. The name is a complete
argument: he was war, he went into exile like Rama, and he is trying to get off
the wheel. **The character's entire backstory is in his name and none of it is
in English.** This is the corpus's purest example of burying the meaning.

**Balaurung** — *A man with the power of dragon.*
`Portmanteau-Root`, mononym. *Balaur* (Romanian: dragon) + **-ung** (Germanic
"scion/descendant of," as in Nibelung, Wölsung). The free bonus: it rhymes with
Glaurung, so a fantasy reader's ear does half the work before they've thought
about it. Mononyms work when the portmanteau is strong enough to stand alone.

**Origajima Foldspire** — *Sorcerer of paper summons and origami.*
`Portmanteau-Root` + `Kenning-Byname` — the layering principle in its cleanest
form. *Origajima* = origami + **-jima** (Japanese island suffix, as in Iwojima):
note it says *origami-island*, a **place**, not a power. Then *Foldspire* says
the power in plain English. Two registers: the name his culture gave him, and
the name people who've seen him fight use. Foldspire beats "Foldmaster" because
*spire* is a structure, not a rank.

**Priest Dondarrion Ravensway** — *Twilight Cleric, Curse of Strahd.*
`High Sindarin (Westerosi cut)` + `Kenning-Byname`. Dondarrion is borrowed
straight from Beric Dondarrion — doubled **rr**, the **-ion** ending, three
syllables of flow. Ravensway pairs an animal with a terrain-noun in the English
surname pattern. *Twilight* cleric, *ravens*, a *way* (a road at dusk): the
byname and the subclass agree without ever saying so.

**Luztherin Theramon** — *Fistfighting martial artist chosen by his deity.*
`Portmanteau-Root` + `High Sindarin (Jordan variant)`. *Luz* (Spanish: light) +
**-therin** lifted from Lews Therin. *Theramon* patterns on Telamon — which in
Greek means a load-bearing column carved as a man. A divinely chosen
fistfighter as a *pillar* is exactly right. This name is the corpus's best
demonstration that a borrowed suffix is not plagiarism; it's register.

**Stranger-Comes-Knocking** — *Inquisitor with eldritch power.*
`Sentence-Name`. Not a description of him — a description of **what happens to
you** when he shows up. Present tense, three words, paintable. The word
*Stranger* refuses to name him, which is the eldritch part; the word *knocking*
is almost polite, which is the terrifying part.

**Xochimeh Tepoch'onnya** — *Tribal druid who turns into hundreds of animals.*
`Apostrophe-Clan`, Mesoamerican variant. *Xochi* (Nahuatl: flower) + **-meh**
(the Nahuatl plural — a druid who is *many*). *Tepoch* (youth) with a glottal
stop before *-onnya*. The apostrophe is a real phoneme here, not decoration,
which is why it reads as a language instead of as a fantasy name.

### Female

**Merveya Ashe'kani** — *Shogun's bodyguard who overthrew the shogun.*
`Portmanteau-Root` + `Apostrophe-Clan`. *Merveille* (French: marvel) + **-eya**.
The apostrophe in Ashe'kani marks a lineage joint — *of the Ashe line, kani
sept* — and *kani* reads Japanese, which matches the shogunate setting. A
bodyguard's name carrying her *house* is thematically exact: she broke the house
she was bound to.

**Norra Kaida** — *Pink-haired bodily-fluids sorceress, perpetually nude.*
`Compact Duotone`. *Norra* (Swedish *norr*, north — cold, clean, austere) +
*Kaida* (Japanese: little dragon — sharp and bright). Two syllables each,
twinned **-a** endings, two phonetic families. The name is small, clean and
tidy, and the character is the opposite of all three — that contrast is the
joke, and it's deliberate.

**Ankara Citra** — *Tribal magician with nature magic.*
`Concept-Loan` in `Compact Duotone` shape. *Ankara* (Turkish city) + *citra*
(Sanskrit: variegated, many-hued, dappled). "Many-hued" **is** nature magic.
Four *a* vowels out of five positions, which makes the name almost a chant.

**Mordekaisa Levisay** — *A general who murdered her own empire to defend her family.*
`Portmanteau-Root`. The author's own gloss: *morde* = murder (Latin *mordere*,
to bite) + *kaisa* = kaisar, emperor. She bit the throat out of an empire. The
trailing *r* of "kaisar" is dropped so the name flows, and the seam is
invisible. *Levisay* is the softer English-shaped family half — the family she
did it all for. **The name contains both halves of her:** what she did, and who
she did it for.

**Seshen Thalion** — *Sorceress who turns into demonic forms.*
`Concept-Loan` + `High Sindarin epithet`. *Seshen* is Egyptian for lotus — the
flower that comes up clean out of mud. *Thalion* is Sindarin for "steadfast,"
taken from Húrin. A soft Egyptian flower-word welded to a hard Elvish
war-epithet *is* a woman who becomes demons and stays herself. The collision
is the characterization.

## Stated influences, and what each one teaches

| Name | Source | The transferable lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Húrin Thalion** | Tolkien | The second name is an *earned epithet*, not a family name. Thalion = "the Steadfast." |
| **Beric Dondarrion** | Martin | Doubled consonants (**rr**) + **-ion** produce nobility cheaply and reliably. |
| **Sand dan Glokta** | Abercrombie | A nobiliary particle inside an *ugly* name says "the aristocracy rotted" in three syllables. |
| **Lord of the Morning** | Martin | A title can *replace* a name. Abstract + time-of-day = ancient office. |
| **Balerion, the Black Dread** | Martin | `[Name], the [Adjective] [Noun]` — and the noun is a *state* (Dread), not a job. |
| **Logen the Bloody Nine** | Abercrombie | The byname can be a **number**. It implies a story you aren't told. Best byname trick in the list. |
| **Galadriel** | Tolkien | *galad* (radiance) + *riel* (garlanded maiden). Meaning first, beauty second — and it's beautiful anyway. |
| **Mandragoran** | Jordan | Take one real, loaded word — *mandragora*, the mandrake that screams — and add one suffix. |
| **Lews Therin Telamon** | Jordan | **Split the given name into two words.** Lews Therin, not Lewstherin. Instant antiquity, zero cost. |
| **Samantha Princesza Jazzyka Engka** | — | A **title used as a middle name** (*Princesza*), plus four parts. A name making a claim about status. |
| **Kaladin Stormblessed** | Sanderson | Plain-English byname where the second half is *gentler* than the first. Not Stormbringer — Storm**blessed**. |

## Patterns across the whole corpus

These are the author's actual preferences, extracted. When a slate feels wrong,
check it against this list.

**1. The meaning is always buried, never stated.** Mordekaisa, not
Murderkaiser. Yuddha Rama Samsara says everything about the character in a
language the table doesn't speak. The one deliberate exception is the
Kenning-Byname layer (Foldspire, Ravensway), which is *supposed* to be the name
the commons use.

**2. Nearly every name fuses two linguistic sources.** Spanish + Jordan-ish.
Egyptian + Sindarin. Nordic + Japanese. French + Japanese. Romanian + Germanic.
Igbo + a Latin suffix. **A monocultural name is the tell for a placeholder.**

**3. The given name is allowed to be ordinary.** Bartholomew. Sand. Norra. The
second name carries the character. Making both halves exotic is the most common
way to overcook one of these.

**4. Real words do the work.** Balaur, seshen, citra, yuddha, merveille, luz,
origami, Chimamanda, kaisar — all real. Pure invention is rare here, and where
it appears (Dalligar) it's still built from real morphemes (**-gar**).

**5. Suffixes are borrowed openly, and that's correct.** **-therin** from
Jordan, **-ion** from Martin and Tolkien, **-ung** from the Nibelungenlied,
**-jima** from Japanese geography. A suffix is a *register marker*, not
someone's property. Borrow the suffix, never the root.

**6. Consonant weight tracks character weight.** Heavy doubled consonants
(Da**ll**igar, Det**ress**chal, Don**darr**ion) for warlocks and clerics; pure
open vowels (Chimamandara) for kings; clipped two-syllable pairs (Norra Kaida)
for fast characters.

**7. Titles are separate and blunt.** *King* Damarion Chimamandara. *Priest*
Dondarrion Ravensway. A short Anglo-Saxon title in front of a long exotic name
anchors it. See `references/epithets.md`.

**8. Female names run shorter and more vowel-final than male ones** in this
corpus — Norra, Kaida, Citra, Seshen, Merveya against Bartholomew, Dondarrion,
Chimamandara, Luztherin. Not a rule to enforce, but worth matching when
generating for an existing party so the set sounds like one world.
