# Path of the Avalanche

*Primal Path — Charger (momentum that builds into an unstoppable force)*

> **An original concept.**
>
> A single stone rolling downhill is nothing — step aside and let it pass. The
> mistake everyone makes is letting the *second* stone follow, and the third,
> and the slope. By the time they understand what's coming, the mountain is
> already moving, and there is no version of "step aside" that works on a
> mountain. That's you. You don't start fights strong. You start them *rolling*.
> Every step you take toward the enemy, every blow that lands, every body that
> tries and fails to stop you — it all adds to the weight behind you, and the
> weight only goes one direction. Hold your ground against an avalanche if you
> like. It will be the last ground you hold.

This path is built around a brand-new resource: **Momentum.** You start each
fight light and grow heavier and faster as you move and strike, until you become a
literally unstoppable force — and once you're rolling, *stopping* you costs your
enemies far more than getting out of the way ever would.

---

## Gathering Momentum (Level 3)

The flavor: the mountain begins to lean. Each stride and each strike adds to the
weight at your back.

You have a pool of **Momentum**, starting at **2** each time you enter a Rage.
Your Momentum can't exceed **6** (this maximum increases at higher levels).

- **Building Speed.** While raging, you gain **1 Momentum** the first time each
  turn you **move at least 15 feet toward an enemy**, and **1 Momentum** the first
  time each turn you **hit a creature with a melee attack** — so moving in *and*
  landing a hit nets 2 in a turn.
- **Weight Behind the Blow (passive).** While raging, you gain a bonus to your
  **melee damage rolls equal to your current Momentum**, and a bonus to your
  **walking Speed equal to 5 × your Momentum** feet. The heavier you get, the
  harder you hit *and* the faster you roll — the avalanche accelerates.
- **Downhill (Bonus Action).** You can **spend Momentum** to surge: move up to
  **10 feet per Momentum spent** in a straight line without provoking Opportunity
  Attacks, and the next melee hit you land before the end of your turn deals an
  extra **1d10 per Momentum spent**.

---

## Unstoppable Force (Level 6)

The flavor: the slope no longer asks your permission. Things in your way are no
longer in your way for very long.

While raging:

- **Can't Be Slowed.** You ignore Difficult Terrain, your Speed can't be reduced
  below your normal Speed, and you have Advantage on saving throws and checks to
  avoid being **Grappled, Restrained, Knocked Prone, or otherwise stopped.** When
  you succeed on such a save, you gain **1 Momentum.**
- **Bowl Through.** When you move, you can move through the space of any creature
  Large or smaller. The first time you enter a creature's space each turn, it
  takes **Bludgeoning damage equal to twice your Momentum + your Rage Damage** and
  must succeed on a Strength save or be knocked **Prone** and shoved aside 5 feet.
- **Rolling Start.** Your maximum Momentum increases to **10.**

---

## Landslide (Level 10)

The flavor: the avalanche stops being a line and becomes a *front*. Everything
downhill of you is in the path.

While raging:

- **Overrun.** Once on each of your turns, when you use **Downhill** or move at
  least 20 feet in a straight line and end next to a creature, each enemy you
  passed within 5 feet of, and the creature you stop beside, takes **Bludgeoning
  damage equal to three times your Momentum** (Strength save halves) and is knocked
  Prone on a failure.
- **No Brakes.** You don't lose Momentum from missing, and you no longer lose
  Momentum at the end of an encounter while you remain raging — it carries from
  one foe to the next. Once per Rage, if you would be reduced to 0 hit points
  while you have 4+ Momentum, you can spend all your Momentum to instead drop to
  **1 hit point.**
- **Greater Weight.** Your maximum Momentum increases to **12.**

---

## Force of Nature (Level 14)

The flavor: there is no longer a meaningful difference between you and a natural
disaster. You have mass, direction, and a complete indifference to being told no.

While raging:

- **Terminal Velocity.** While you have **maximum Momentum**, your melee attacks
  ignore **Resistance**, score critical hits on a roll of **19 or 20**, and you
  can't be moved, stopped, or have your Speed reduced by **any** effect — magical
  or otherwise. You move in straight lines through walls of force, webs, and the
  like, ending such effects you pass through.
- **The Whole Mountain (the button, once per Rage).** When you have **6+
  Momentum**, you can spend it all to **charge through the battlefield**: move up
  to **double your Speed** in any path (it need not be straight), passing through
  creatures and unattended structures. Each creature whose space you enter takes
  **2d10 Bludgeoning per point of Momentum spent** (Strength save for half) and is
  knocked Prone on a failure, and walls or barriers you pass through are
  demolished. You can't be targeted by Opportunity Attacks during this movement.
- **Unceasing.** When you reduce a creature to 0 hit points, you immediately gain
  **1 Momentum** (this can exceed your maximum until the end of your turn) — the
  avalanche only grows.

---

### Designer notes

- **Momentum** is the original mechanic: a per-Rage pool, capped **6 → 10 → 12**,
  that you build by *playing like an avalanche* (moving in and hitting) and spend
  on burst movement and damage. It scales both your **damage** and your **Speed**
  simultaneously, so an Avalanche that's been allowed to roll is fast *and*
  brutal — and slow to get going, which is the intended weakness.
- The path answers the Barbarian's classic problem (kiting, control, forced
  movement): by level 6 you have **Advantage to shrug off** the lockdown suite
  (and gain Momentum for doing so), and you **plow through** anything in your way
  for damage.
- **The Whole Mountain** is the once-per-Rage payoff for building a full pool — a
  board-crossing, save-for-half trample that demolishes terrain. It's gated behind
  *having spent the fight building up*, so it rewards the play pattern instead of
  being a turn-one nuke. Raise the per-point dice to 2d10 if it underwhelms at
  your table.
- Tuning lever: if tracking Momentum every turn is too fiddly, run it as "gain 1
  per turn you move-and-hit, lose nothing, cap at 10," and ignore the
  save-on-stop and kill triggers.
