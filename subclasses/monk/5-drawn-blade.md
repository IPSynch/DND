# Warrior of the Drawn Blade

*Monk Subclass*

Other monks made peace with the empty hand. You made peace with steel. To you a sword is
not a weapon held apart from the body but an extension of it — three of them, if your
discipline runs deep enough, one in each hand and one clenched in your teeth. You move
through a fight the way water finds the low ground: every cut a single unbroken motion,
every guard already a counter, every breath a step closer to the one stroke that cleaves
whatever stands before you, be it flesh, steel, or stone.

*Throughout these features, your **Monk DC** equals 8 + your Proficiency Bonus + your
Wisdom modifier, and "Martial Arts die" refers to your current Martial Arts die. You
spend **Focus Points** (called Ki in some traditions).*

## Level 3: Bladed Discipline

The sword and the open hand were never two different arts.

***Swordsman's Forms.*** You gain proficiency with all martial weapons that deal Slashing
damage, and such weapons count as Monk weapons for you regardless of their other
properties, letting you use Dexterity, your Martial Arts die, Flurry of Blows, and your
other Monk features with them. You also ignore the Two-Handed and Heavy properties of
those weapons.

***Three-Sword Style.*** You can wield a weapon in each hand and a third in your teeth (or
with a tail, prehensile grip, or similar). When you take the Attack action, you can make
one extra attack with a different weapon as a Bonus Action without the usual penalty for
attacking with two weapons, and you add your ability modifier to that attack's damage.

***Iron-Cutter.*** Your weapon attacks ignore Resistance to Slashing damage and can damage
objects and barriers as though you wielded a siege weapon.

## Level 6: Flowing Steel

A guard is only a counter you haven't thrown yet.

***Air Slash.*** When you take the Attack action, one of your weapon attacks can become a
ranged slash, striking a target within 30 feet of you or every creature in a 15-foot-long
Line (make one attack roll against each).

***Parry.*** When a creature you can see hits you with an attack, you can take a Reaction
and spend 1 Focus Point to add your Martial Arts die plus your Wisdom modifier to your
Armor Class against that attack, potentially turning the hit into a miss. If the attack
misses as a result, you can immediately make one weapon attack against the attacker.

***Asura Pressure.*** While you wield two or more weapons, creatures have Disadvantage on
saving throws against your Stunning Strike, and you can use Stunning Strike with weapon
attacks.

## Level 11: Three Thousand Worlds

Three blades, one will — and for an instant the foe is facing a crowd of you.

***Master Swordsman.*** Your weapon attacks score a critical hit on a roll of 19–20, and
your Three-Sword Style now lets you make two extra weapon attacks as the Bonus Action
instead of one.

***Onigiri.*** As part of the Attack action, you can spend 2 Focus Points to unleash a
whirling three-blade cut. Each creature in a 15-foot Cone or a 30-foot-long Line takes
Slashing damage equal to three rolls of your Martial Arts die plus your Wisdom modifier
(make one damage roll for all of them), or half as much on a successful Dexterity saving
throw. A creature that fails is also knocked Prone.

***Unbreakable Stance.*** When you are reduced to 0 Hit Points but not killed outright, you
can spend 1 Focus Point to drop to 1 Hit Point instead. You can do so once per Short or
Long Rest.

## Level 17: Nine-Sword Style — Asura

Nine blades, three faces, six arms wreathed in killing intent. For a few breaths, the
swordsman becomes the demon the legends warned of.

As a Bonus Action, you can spend 5 Focus Points to enter the Asura stance. This stance
lasts for 1 minute. It ends early if you fall Unconscious or if you end it (no action
required). While in the Asura stance:

- You manifest phantom arms and blades: when you take the Attack action, you make three
  additional weapon attacks, and your reach with weapon attacks increases by 10 feet.
- Your weapon attacks deal extra damage equal to two rolls of your Martial Arts die
  (Force) and score a critical hit on a roll of 18–20.
- You have Resistance to all damage and can't gain the Frightened condition.
- ***King of Hell.*** Once on each of your turns, you can spend your action on a single
  annihilating stroke against a creature you can see. That creature and each creature in a
  20-foot-wide, 60-foot-long Line extending behind it takes 10d12 Slashing damage, or half
  as much on a successful Constitution saving throw. Nonmagical walls, gates, and lesser
  barriers in the Line are cut in two.

When the Asura stance ends, you have one level of Exhaustion — the body pays for wearing
the demon's shape.

---

### Designer Notes

- This subclass makes a weapon-using Monk fully native to the 2024 chassis rather than
  bolted on. Folding Slashing martials into Monk weapons and stripping Two-Handed and
  Heavy lets a greatsword-style blade run on Dexterity and the Martial Arts die.
- Three-Sword Style's scaling (one → two → three-plus extra attacks) is the engine, and
  it stacks with Flurry of Blows for genuinely absurd high-level turns — the intended
  ceiling. If King of Hell's "cut any barrier in two" trivializes your set-pieces,
  restrict it to nonmagical barriers (as written) or require a check against your Monk DC.
